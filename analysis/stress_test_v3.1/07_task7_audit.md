# Task 7: Circularity and logic audit

Ranked by damage to credibility. This is a stress test of v3.1 as
published. No default was changed.

## 1. The 0% coverage default is chosen to match the launch (high)

The mid-band 100% 1P unit result at A is implied by seller coverage of
0% plus a positive 1P contribution. When coverage is 0%, the 3P payoff
is identically zero, so any positive 1P payoff (contribution plus
spillover, and even contribution alone) wins. That is the right
alternative *if* coverage really is zero. Task 0 finds that 0% is
evidenced at the March 2026 launch and is not replaced by a later
published share, so the input is defensible. It is also, plainly,
chosen to match Joybuy's 1P-only launch.

The "not fitted" claim is technically true: no parameter was searched
to hit 100% 1P. A 5% haircut, variable-only fulfilment and 8%
spillover were justified on other grounds, and spillover is not
needed for the default result. The honest wording is: the model was
not calibrated by a search over parameters, but the coverage default
was set to the launch-day state, and that single choice does most of
the work on the unit 100% 1P result. A reviewer who hears "not fitted"
may think the 100% came out as a surprise. It should not have.

## 2. B's 25% / 6% 1P headline rests on the THG (and Zalando) proxies (high)

A 5-point cut in beauty and clothing established gross margin, which
the brief asked for because THG Beauty and Zalando are own-brand and
DTC-heavy, turns all five B 1P lines (four beauty, baby care) from
Bring in-house to Test as 3P first. Mid-band 1P at B becomes 0 of 20
and 0% of modelled sales. That is not a small sensitivity. The
published 39.3% (THG FY2025) and 39.9% (Zalando FY2025) are real
filings, but they are used as if they were the margin on third-party
brand resale in a UK marketplace 1P programme. They may overstate it.
The established 1P mix is assumption-driven.

## 3. The 5% haircut is a UK Assumption dressed with a China filing (medium)

JD 20-F FY2025, Item 4.B, pages 88 and 95, does say JD has supplier
and manufacturer relationships and can obtain better procurement
terms. Joybuy is not named in the 20-F. The step from those sentences
to "a 5% residual UK haircut, not 25%" is a judgement, not a
measurement. At a 13.52% haircut the first mid-band unit verdict
leaves 1P (pushchairs, Do not range). The operating layer still
ranges it. The 5% is inside the 0–10% band that was pre-committed;
it was not searched to keep unit 1P intact. It should still be
labelled as a UK-local Assumption, not as a figure "from the 20-F".

## 4. The 50% strategic threshold has no citation (medium, and not binding here)

The land-grab layer follows unit economics at or above 50% coverage,
an Assumption with no paper or filing behind it. In this run it does
not decide the headline: at default 8% spillover, unit and operating
leave 100% 1P together, at 72.3% coverage, which is already above
50%. The threshold would matter if unit flipped earlier. It is still
arbitrary and should be said to be.

## 5. Spillover is applied in full to 1P and only in part to 3P (medium, not binding at A)

The 8% basket spillover is an Assumption. It is added in full to the
1P payoff and only with coverage to the 3P payoff, which is
consistent with the "hole in the range" story. It is not needed for
A's default 100% 1P (spillover 0% still 20/20 at 0% coverage). It
does move close B clothing verdicts (footwear flips at 1.1%
spillover; sportswear at 10.4%; everyday at 18.3%).

## 6. A single mid-band unit ignores cannibalisation, the buy-box, seller churn, and the long tail the model already has (medium)

v3.1 already evaluates three price bands. On all 60 bands, five
low-velocity high-price bands are Do not range at A (cooking,
office, outerwear, pushchairs, car seats). The mid-band 20/20 is a
statement about the mid band, not about every SKU. The model also
discloses that it does not treat cannibalisation, buy-box rules or
a seller's decision to leave. Those limits are real. Task 6's
request to rebuild SKU curves belongs in v3.2, not in a critique of
the published mid-band tables.

## 7. Using Amazon's 61% as a tuning target would itself be circular (lower, because we did not)

The brief asked for a calibration of B against 61% 3P worldwide
units. That figure is a different geography, a different mix, and
units not sales. This stress test reports only direction (B is
1P-light for these five UK categories) and does not tune. If v3.2
were to raise B's 1P sales share toward 39%, that would be fitting.
